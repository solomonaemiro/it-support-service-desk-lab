#Skills Demonstrated

##Overview

The osTicket IT Support Service Desk Lab provided practical experience across Service Desk operations, Windows troubleshooting, network diagnostics, user support, knowledge management, and technical documentation.

The skills demonstrated through the project are grouped into six areas: Service Desk Operations, Windows Troubleshooting, Network Troubleshooting, User Support, Knowledge Management, and Technical Documentation.

---

##Service Desk Operations

###Ticket Management

Created, investigated, updated, resolved, and closed support tickets using osTicket.

Demonstrated understanding of the ticket lifecycle from initial submission through final closure.

###Ticket Triage

Reviewed reported symptoms and categorized incidents according to the affected system or service.

###Ticket Assignment

Demonstrated manual ticket assignment to a support technician when individual ownership was required.

Depending on the Service Desk workflow, tickets may also remain routed to a department or team queue until a technician takes ownership.

###Priority Management

Applied appropriate ticket priorities based on the nature of the reported issue.

###SLA Awareness

Worked with SLA information and ticket due dates to understand expected service timelines.

###Help Topics

Used Help Topics to categorize incoming support requests and organize the Service Desk workflow.

###Internal Notes

Documented technical investigation, diagnostic findings, actions taken, and resolution details using internal ticket notes.

###Customer Communication

Provided customer-facing responses using clear, user-friendly language appropriate for end users.

###Ticket Closure

Closed incidents after the reported problem had been resolved and functionality verified.

Used Windows Settings to investigate and modify system configuration, including microphone privacy permissions and language and keyboard settings.

---

###Network Troubleshooting

The project included a practical network troubleshooting scenario involving Wi-Fi and Internet access.

IP Configuration

Used:

ipconfig /all

to examine the workstation's IPv4 address, subnet information, default gateway, DHCP information, and DNS configuration.

DNS Troubleshooting

Used:

nslookup google.com

to test hostname resolution and examine DNS behavior.

Connectivity Testing

Used:

ping google.com

to test external connectivity and packet loss.

Evidence-Based Diagnosis

Interpreted network diagnostic results rather than assuming that a reported Internet problem automatically indicated a DNS or network configuration failure.

The investigation demonstrated the importance of distinguishing between the user's reported symptom and the actual condition observed during technical testing.

###Transferable IT Support Skills

The project also demonstrates several skills that transfer directly to a professional Service Desk environment.

Problem Solving

Breaking unclear or complex technical problems into smaller, testable components.

Troubleshooting Methodology

Following a structured troubleshooting process rather than applying random fixes.

Evidence-Based Diagnosis

Using observable technical evidence to narrow down possible causes and determine appropriate actions.

Attention to Detail

Recording relevant technical information and documenting troubleshooting actions accurately.

Communication

Explaining technical issues and solutions in language appropriate for end users.

Documentation

Creating useful records that support future troubleshooting, knowledge sharing, and service continuity.

Verification

Testing the original user-facing problem after applying a resolution rather than assuming that a configuration change was successful.

Ownership

Following incidents through investigation, resolution, documentation, customer communication, and closure when assigned responsibility for the ticket.
